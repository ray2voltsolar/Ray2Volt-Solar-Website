Behind the meter: which side of the meter is the plant on?

The utility meter is the DISCOM's meter at your connection, where the public network ends and your premises begin. A plant on your side of it is “behind the meter”: its power is used on the spot. A plant on the network's side is “in front of the meter”: its power must travel to reach anyone.

Why behind the meter is worth more per unit

Each unit (kilowatt-hour) used directly is one unit you do not buy from the DISCOM at the retail tariff, the full price per unit on your bill. It never touches the network, so no transmission or wheeling charges apply and nothing is lost in the wires.

Why front-of-meter power costs more to deliver

It has to travel: over high-voltage transmission lines, through the DISCOM's local network (“wheeling”), then through the buyer's meter. Some power is lost on the way, and the network owners charge for carrying it. Rates are set by each State Electricity Regulatory Commission.

Zero export

Solar that never leaves the building

A reverse-power relay watches the connection and stops any power flowing out to the grid. Chosen where the building uses nearly all its solar in the day, or where export arrangements are not wanted. In Andhra Pradesh, zero-export behind-the-meter systems follow a simplified intimation process.

Behind-the-meter batteries

Midday sunshine, used in the evening

A battery on your side of the meter stores surplus solar at midday and releases it in the evening, so more of each day's solar is used on site. Illustrative shapes, not to scale.
