Behind the meter Which side of the meter is the plant on?

The utility meter marks where the DISCOM's network ends and a consumer's premises begin. A plant on the consumer's side is behind the meter: its power is used where it is made. A plant on the network's side is in front of the meter: its power must travel to reach anyone.

Why the side matters

A unit used on site replaces a unit bought at the full price

Each unit (kilowatt-hour) used directly is one unit not bought from the DISCOM at the retail tariff, the full price per unit on the bill. It never enters the network, so no transmission or wheeling (local network) charges apply and nothing is lost in the wires.

Front-of-the-meter power has to travel, so it pays for the journey: network charges, plus losses along the way.

Zero export

Solar that never leaves the building

A reverse-power relay detects power starting to flow out to the grid and stops it. Zero export suits buildings that use nearly all their solar in the day, or owners who do not want an export arrangement. In Andhra Pradesh, zero-export behind-the-meter systems follow a simplified intimation process.
