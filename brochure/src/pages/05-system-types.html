On-grid, off-grid and hybrid: three kinds of solar system

Every solar plant has panels and an inverter, which turns the panels' direct current into the alternating current a building uses. What differs is whether it is connected to the grid (the network of your DISCOM, the local electricity distribution company) and whether it has a battery.

The part that surprises most people

Why does an on-grid system switch off in a power cut?

To protect the linemen who repair the wires. When the grid fails, they work on lines they expect to be dead. If rooftop plants kept feeding power in, a “dead” wire could be live. So grid-connected inverters must switch off automatically. This is called anti-islanding: the plant may not become a powered “island” on a dead network.

  1. The grid supply fails.
  2. The inverter senses it and switches itself off.
  3. No power reaches the lines being repaired, even in full sun.
