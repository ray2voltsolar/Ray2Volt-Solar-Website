On-grid, off-grid and hybrid Three kinds of solar system

Every plant has panels and an inverter, which turns the panels' direct current into the alternating current a building uses. What differs is the link to the grid (the network of your DISCOM, the local distribution company) and the battery.

The point that surprises most people

Why does an on-grid plant switch off in a power cut?

To protect the linemen who repair the network: they work on lines they expect to be dead. So grid-connected inverters must switch off automatically when the grid fails, rather than become a powered “island” on a dead network. This rule is called anti-islanding.

  1. The grid supply fails.
  2. The inverter senses it and switches off.
  3. No power reaches the lines under repair, even in full sun.
