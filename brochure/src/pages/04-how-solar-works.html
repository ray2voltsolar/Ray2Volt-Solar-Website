How solar works From sunlight to the socket

Solar panels turn daylight into electricity, with no moving parts and no fuel. An inverter converts it into the form appliances use, and the building uses it first. The grid stays connected alongside: it takes any surplus and supplies whatever solar cannot.

The surplus

Around midday a plant usually makes more than the building needs. That surplus flows out to the grid through the meter.

The shortfall

Mornings, evenings and nights, the building draws from the grid. How the two are settled on the bill is the subject of pages 07 to 09.

kW and units

kW is the rate of power at a moment (the height of the curve). A unit, 1 kWh, is energy over time (the area beneath it).
