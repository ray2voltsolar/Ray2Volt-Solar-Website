Metering  ·  Net metering
How solar is billed · Option 1

Net metering: one meter that counts both ways

Your solar plant powers your building first. Whatever you don't use at that moment flows out to the grid, and whatever you still need comes in from it. A single two-way meter keeps count, and you pay only for the difference — the “net”.

How the power flows
1Solar first. Sunlight becomes electricity and runs your own lights and machines.
2Surplus out. Extra solar flows to the grid. The meter records it as export.
3Top-up in. When solar is not enough, grid power comes in as import.

Without solar this home would be billed for all 500 units. With net metering it is billed for 140. Each exported unit cancels one imported unit at the full retail rate, which is why net metering usually gives the biggest saving per unit.

If you export more than you import

The surplus is not lost. Depending on your state, it is carried forward as credit to the next bill or paid to you at a rate fixed by the state regulator, usually lower than the retail tariff.

Limits to know

Every State Electricity Regulatory Commission sets its own size limits, settlement period and surplus rate. Plant size is normally capped by your sanctioned load. In Andhra Pradesh, new net-metered systems can be up to 500 kW, settled monthly.

Usually suits

Anyone who uses a good share of their solar power themselves.
