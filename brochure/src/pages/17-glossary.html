Glossary The words, in plain English

The terms used in this guide, A to Z. The rules behind many of them are set state by state.

ChapterBasicsMeteringOpen accessMoney36 terms
A
Additional surcharge
Open-access charge covering the DISCOM’s fixed costs for power already contracted for you.
ALMM
Approved List of Models and Manufacturers: the government’s list of approved solar panels.
Anti-islanding
Switches an on-grid inverter off when the grid fails, so no power reaches lines under repair.
B
Banking
Storing surplus power with the DISCOM to draw later in the same billing cycle, less a charge.
Behind the meter
On the consumer’s side of the meter, serving the consumer’s own load first.
BOOT
Build-own-operate-transfer: like RESCO, but the customer takes ownership at the end of the term.
C
CAPEX
You pay for and own the plant, keep the savings and handle upkeep.
Captive
Open access from your own plant: at least 26% ownership, and 51% of its yearly output used.
Contract demand / sanctioned load
The maximum load a connection is approved for; it normally caps plant size.
Cross-subsidy surcharge
Open-access charge compensating the DISCOM, as large consumers’ tariffs subsidise others.
D
DCR
Domestic content requirement: subsidised panels, and from June 2026 their cells, must be made in India.
DISCOM
Distribution company: supplies and bills your electricity.
F
Feed-in tariff
Per-unit rate paid for solar power sent to the grid, set by the SERC.
Front of the meter
Plants that feed the grid directly, such as solar parks and open-access plants.
G
Green tariff
A rate set by the SERC at which the DISCOM supplies green power on request.
Gross metering
All solar output sold to the DISCOM at a feed-in tariff; own use billed separately.
Group captive
Consumers who together hold at least 26% of a plant and use at least 51% of its output.
Group net metering
One plant’s surplus credited to the same consumer’s other connections in the DISCOM area.
H
Hybrid system
Solar with a battery that keeps chosen loads running in a power cut.
I
Inverter
Converts the panels’ DC power to AC for the building and the grid.
K
kW
Kilowatt: the size or power of a plant, often written kWp.
kWh / unit
Kilowatt-hour, the “unit” on a bill: 1,000 watts used for one hour.
N
Net billing
Settled in money: imports at the retail tariff, exports at the lower feed-in tariff.
Net metering
One two-way meter; exported units offset imported ones and the net is billed.
O
Off-grid
A system with no connection to the DISCOM’s grid at all.
On-grid
Connected to the grid; switches off in a power cut (see anti-islanding).
Open access
The right to use the grid to buy power from another seller, or from your own plant elsewhere, for regulated charges.
OPEX / RESCO
A developer owns and runs the plant on your site; you pay per unit used, at an agreed rate.
P
PPA
Power purchase agreement: a long-term contract to buy power at an agreed per-unit rate.
R
RCO
Renewable Consumption Obligation: a minimum renewable share required of large consumers and DISCOMs.
REC
Renewable Energy Certificate: a tradable certificate for 1 MWh (1,000 units) of renewable power.
S
SERC
State Electricity Regulatory Commission: sets tariffs and rules. In Andhra Pradesh, APERC.
V
Virtual net metering
One shared plant’s output credited to several consumers in agreed shares, such as flat owners.
Virtual PPA
A financial contract backing a renewable plant for its green certificates, without taking its power.
W
Wheeling
Carrying power over the DISCOM’s local network, for a wheeling charge.
Z
Zero export
A plant with a reverse-power relay, so nothing flows to the grid.
