Glossary The words, in plain English

The terms used in this guide, A to Z. The rules behind many of them are set state by state.

ChapterBasicsMeteringOpen accessMoney36 terms
A
Additional surcharge
Open-access charge covering the DISCOM’s fixed costs for power it had already contracted for you.
ALMM
Approved List of Models and Manufacturers: the government’s list of approved solar panels.
Anti-islanding
Safety feature that switches an on-grid inverter off when the grid fails, so no power reaches lines under repair.
B
Banking
“Depositing” surplus power with the DISCOM and drawing it later in the same billing cycle, less a charge.
Behind the meter
Generation or storage on the consumer’s side of the meter, serving the consumer’s own load first.
BOOT
Build-own-operate-transfer: like RESCO, but the plant passes to the customer at the end of the term.
C
CAPEX
The customer pays for and owns the plant, keeps the savings and is responsible for upkeep.
Captive
Open access from your own plant: at least 26% ownership, and at least 51% of its output used each year.
Contract demand / sanctioned load
The maximum load a connection is approved for. Plant size is normally capped by it.
Cross-subsidy surcharge
Open-access charge that compensates the DISCOM, because large consumers’ tariffs help subsidise others.
D
DCR
Domestic content requirement: a subsidy-linked rule that panels, and from June 2026 their cells, be made in India.
DISCOM
Distribution company: the company that supplies and bills your electricity.
F
Feed-in tariff
The per-unit rate paid for solar power sent to the grid, set by the SERC.
Front of the meter
Plants that feed the grid directly, such as solar parks and open-access plants.
G
Green tariff
A rate set by the SERC at which the DISCOM supplies green power to consumers who ask for it.
Gross metering
All solar output is sold to the DISCOM at a feed-in tariff; the building’s own use is billed separately.
Group captive
Several consumers together hold at least 26% of a plant and use at least 51% of its output.
Group net metering
Surplus from one consumer’s plant is credited to that consumer’s other connections in the same DISCOM area.
H
Hybrid system
Solar with a battery, which keeps chosen loads running in a power cut.
I
Inverter
Converts the panels’ DC power into AC power for the building and the grid.
K
kW
Kilowatt: the size or power of a plant, often written kWp.
kWh / unit
Kilowatt-hour, the “unit” on a bill: a 1,000-watt appliance running for one hour.
N
Net billing
Imports and exports settled in money: imports at the retail tariff, exports at the lower feed-in tariff.
Net metering
One two-way meter. Exported units offset imported units, and only the net is billed.
O
Off-grid
A system with no connection to the DISCOM’s grid at all.
On-grid
A system connected to the grid. It switches off in a power cut (see anti-islanding).
Open access
The right to use the grid to buy power from a seller other than your DISCOM, or to carry power from your own plant elsewhere, for regulated charges.
OPEX / RESCO
A developer owns and runs the plant on your site; you pay only for the units you use, at an agreed rate.
P
PPA
Power purchase agreement: a long-term contract to buy electricity at an agreed per-unit rate.
R
RCO
Renewable Consumption Obligation: a minimum share of renewable power required of large consumers and DISCOMs.
REC
Renewable Energy Certificate: a tradable certificate for 1 MWh (1,000 units) of renewable power.
S
SERC
State Electricity Regulatory Commission: sets tariffs and rules in each state. In Andhra Pradesh, APERC.
V
Virtual net metering
One shared plant’s output credited to several consumers in agreed shares, such as residents of a block of flats.
Virtual PPA
A financial contract to support a renewable plant and receive its green certificates, without taking its power.
W
Wheeling
Carrying power over the DISCOM’s local network; the wheeling charge pays for it.
Z
Zero export
A plant fitted with a reverse-power relay so that nothing flows to the grid.
