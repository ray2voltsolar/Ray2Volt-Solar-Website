Ways to own a solar plant: who pays, who owns, who saves

There are two main routes. In CAPEX (capital expenditure) the customer buys the plant and keeps all the savings. In OPEX or RESCO (Renewable Energy Service Company), a developer pays for a plant on the customer’s roof; the customer pays only for the units it uses.

Two variations

Lease. The customer pays a fixed monthly rent for the system; the company renting it out (the lessor) owns it.

BOOT (build, own, operate, transfer). Like RESCO, but ownership of the plant passes to the customer at the end of the term.

The same model, seen from the investor’s side

Where the income comes from

Under RESCO the investor pays for the plant. Its income is the per-unit payments the power user makes over the contract, which for business rooftops typically runs 15–25 years.

The main risks

  • The user’s ability to pay for the whole term
  • Plant performance over the years
  • Regulation, which can change
