Green energy: what it is and why it matters

Green energy is electricity from sources that nature keeps replenishing and that emit little or no carbon dioxide (CO₂) while running: solar, wind, hydro and biomass, plus storage or green hydrogen charged from them. Fossil power burns coal, gas or oil, which are used up as they burn.

Two ways to make the same electricity
India’s position · power capacity, in gigawatts (GW)

1 GW = 1,000,000 kW. India ranks 3rd in the world in total renewable energy capacity (IRENA 2026). The 500 GW target counts all non-fossil sources, including large hydro and nuclear. Timeline not to scale.

Why it matters · 1

Steadier costs

Sunlight and wind come with no fuel bill. Once a plant is built, the cost of each unit it makes does not rise and fall with coal or gas prices.

Why it matters · 2

Cleaner air, lower emissions

Each unit of solar used in place of grid power avoids roughly the grid’s average emissions. 1 kW of solar avoids about 1 tonne of CO₂ a year.

0.68kg CO₂per unit, India’s grid average (Central Electricity Authority, FY 2025-26)

Why it matters · 3

Energy security

Sunshine and wind are home-grown. Power made on your own roof is used where it is made, with no fuel to mine or ship, and less lost along the wires.
