Gross metering Sell everything, buy everything

Every unit the solar plant generates is sold to the DISCOM, your local electricity distribution company, through a meter of its own. Every unit the building uses is bought from the grid as before. The two are settled separately: one is a bill, the other is income.

The feed-in tariff

The rate per unit paid for solar power sent to the grid. It is set by the State Electricity Regulatory Commission and usually fixed for the agreement term. It is usually lower than the retail tariff, which also pays for the wires and services that bring power to the door.

Why it can still appeal

  • A fixed, predictable income for the agreement term.
  • Income does not depend on how much power the building uses.
  • Plants can be larger than net-metering limits allow.

Where it is typically used

Large roofs with little daytime use, plants owned by investors, and systems above net-metering limits. In Andhra Pradesh it is available for larger systems, up to 5 MW. Confirm current rules with your DISCOM before you decide.

Illustrative example: ₹7 a unit from the grid, ₹3 a unit for exports. Real tariffs have slabs and fixed charges, and export rates are set by each state.
