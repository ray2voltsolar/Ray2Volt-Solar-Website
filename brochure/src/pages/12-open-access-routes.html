Open access: three routes, and what the journey costs

You can own the plant, share one with others, or simply buy its power. The route decides which charges you pay on the way from the plant to your meter.

Three routes: who owns the plant, who uses the power

Percentages in the dials are illustrative; the red tick marks each legal test. In group captive, each user's share of the power must be in proportion to its shareholding. All three routes pay transmission and wheeling charges, losses, banking and scheduling charges.

Banking terms

Same billing cycleBanked units are drawn back within the cycle; national rules allow banking at least monthly.
A cap on the amountAt least 30% of your monthly consumption from the DISCOM. Andhra Pradesh: up to 30%, within the monthly cycle.
Charge taken in kindA share of the banked units is kept. Andhra Pradesh: 8%.
Use it or lose itUnused banked energy is generally not carried forward. Terms vary by state.

Who it suits

Typically viable for consumers with large, steady daytime demand.

Savings

Savings depend on state charges, voltage level and consumption pattern; ask for a site-specific estimate.

Commitment

Long-term agreements, often 10 years or more. Confirm current rules with your DISCOM before you decide.
