Open access  /  How it works
Buying green power at scale

Open access Buying power from someone other than your local supplier

Normally a site buys all its power from the DISCOM, its local distribution company. Open access lets a large consumer buy from another seller, or its own distant plant, over the existing grid. Like a courier: you choose the seller, the network delivers, you pay for delivery.

National rules and state regulations allow consumers with 100 kW or more of sanctioned load to buy green power through open access, on terms set by each state.

Sanctioned load: the maximum load a connection is approved for. Several connections in one DISCOM division can add up to 100 kW.

How it differs from rooftop solar

The plant need not be on your premises

Rooftop solarOpen access
Plant locationYour own roofAnywhere on the grid
Size set byRoof area, about 10 m² per kWYour demand
Grid chargesNone on power used on sitePaid on each unit delivered

Useful when a site needs far more power than its roof can produce. Typically viable for consumers with large, steady daytime demand.

One national portal

Apply through greenopenaccess.in, a single-window portal run by Grid-India; state nodal agencies process applications.

The alternative: a green tariff

Consumers who cannot or prefer not to use open access can ask the DISCOM for green power at a “green tariff” set by the state regulator.
